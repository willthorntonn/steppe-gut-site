# 02 — Brand Guidelines (website application)

> This document does **not** restate `BRAND_GUIDELINES.md` at the project root. That file is brand truth — colour values, logo lockups, typefaces, product facts, ingredients, regulatory data. Read it first. This document covers what that file does not: **how the brand behaves in website copy and website design decisions.**

---

## 1. The brand in one paragraph

Steppe Gut is a fermented mare's milk supplement from Mongolia, sold in Thailand as a daily ingestible for skin and gut. It is positioned as *Functional Luxury* — a small, expensive, unglamorous-looking sachet that a busy woman takes every morning because it works, not because it performs wellness at her. The tagline is *Natural Radiance from Within*.

---

## 2. Audience

**Primary: Thai women, 28–45, affluent, urban.**

What matters about them for copy decisions:

| Trait | What it means for the writing |
|---|---|
| Time-poor, high-responsibility | Front-load the point. No throat-clearing paragraphs. Every section should be comprehensible from its heading and first sentence alone |
| Already spends on skincare and wellness | They are not price-sensitive; they are *credibility*-sensitive. They have been marketed to expertly and are fluent in the tells |
| Has never heard of fermented mare's milk | Assume zero category knowledge, never assume low intelligence. Explain the noun before you praise it |
| Trusts science, distrusts hype | Cite mechanism, name limits, say "we don't know yet" where true. This is the highest-leverage trust move available |
| Bilingual, reads Thai by preference | English copy must survive translation. Avoid idiom, wordplay, cultural references and puns |

**Secondary audiences:** trade buyers (`/stockists/`), press (`/press/`), prospective employees (`/careers/`). Each gets a page written in the same voice, not a corporate register.

---

## 3. Voice

Seven adjectives, in priority order when they conflict:

1. **Understated** — the most important one. If a sentence could be quieter and still be clear, make it quieter.
2. **Educational** — explain first, always.
3. **Warm** — a person is writing, not a department.
4. **Premium** — through restraint and precision, never through adjectives.
5. **Trustworthy** — say what is known, and say what isn't.
6. **Simple** — short sentences. Plain words. Grade 8–9 reading level in English.
7. **Professional** — accurate, correctly punctuated, never sloppy.

### 3.1 The voice test

Before shipping any sentence, ask: *would a thoughtful person who knows this subject well say this out loud to a friend?* If it only works as marketing, rewrite it.

### 3.2 Sentence-level rules

- Average sentence length **14–18 words**. Vary it; never let three long sentences run together.
- Paragraphs **max 3 sentences** on marketing pages, max 5 on science pages.
- **Active voice** by default. Passive is allowed where the actor genuinely doesn't matter ("The milk is fermented for four days").
- **No exclamation marks.** None. Anywhere on the site.
- **One idea per sentence.**
- Use **em-dashes sparingly** — at most one per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete nouns to abstract ones: *sachet*, *mare*, *four days*, *ten grams* — not *solution*, *journey*, *experience*, *wellness ecosystem*.
- Numbers: spell out one to nine, use digits from 10. Always digits for measurements (`10 g`, `100 ml`, `90 capsules`).

## 4. Vocabulary

### 4.1 Words the brand uses

fermented · mare's milk · steppe · herder · ger · Mongolia · daily · ritual · nourish · gut · microbiome · lactoferrin · bioavailable · traditional · sourced · batch · sachet · radiance · calm · consistent · plainly · we don't yet know

### 4.2 Words the brand never uses

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| superfood, miracle, magic, secret, ancient secret | Category hype. Instantly cheapens a premium product |
| detox, cleanse, flush, purify | Pseudoscience. Also a regulatory hazard |
| boost, supercharge, turbocharge, optimise | Vague and unsubstantiated |
| game-changer, revolutionary, breakthrough, disrupt | Corporate |
| journey, unlock, elevate, curated, bespoke, artisanal | Luxury-marketing filler that says nothing |
| exotic, mystical, tribal, primitive, nomad wisdom | Orientalising. Mongolia is a real country with a real dairy industry, not a mood board |
| clinically proven, doctor recommended, #1 | Unsubstantiated claims. See §6 |
| Limited time, hurry, don't miss out, selling fast | Urgency is off-brand and, pre-launch, dishonest |
| synergy, holistic wellness ecosystem, 360° | Meaningless |

### 4.3 Product naming

Always **Steppe Gut** (two words, both capitalised). Never *SteppeGut*, *Steppe-Gut*, *STEPPE GUT* in body copy (the wordmark is a logo, not a typographic style for prose).

Formats are named: **Daily Sachets**, **Capsules**, **Refill Pouch**. Never "SKU", "variant" or "product 1" in user-facing copy.

The ingredient is **fermented mare's milk**, lower case, always with "fermented". Never "horse milk". "Kumis" may be used once per page as a parenthetical historical reference, never as the product name.

## 5. How Mongolia is handled

This is the brand's largest asset and its largest tonal risk.

**Do:**
- State provenance as fact: "Product of Mongolia." "The milk is collected in Töv Province between June and October."
- Give herders agency and specificity — they are partners and skilled producers, not scenery.
- Explain *why* Mongolia matters materially: mares are milked seasonally, on open grassland, and the practice of fermenting mare's milk there is continuous and everyday, not revived.
- Use real seasonal and practical detail. Specificity is what makes provenance credible.

**Don't:**
- Use Mongolia as an aesthetic. No "ancient wisdom", no "nomadic secrets", no shamanic register.
- Imply that tradition alone proves efficacy. Tradition explains *why the ingredient exists*; research explains *whether it does anything*. Keep those two arguments separate — conflating them is the single most common failure in this category.
- Show Mongolian people as anonymous silhouettes or props. If a person appears in an image, they are doing something specific and skilled.
- Translate Mongolian words for flavour. `ger` is fine because it's the correct word for the dwelling; anything beyond that is decoration.

## 6. Claim discipline — the hard constraint

Steppe Gut is launching **before Thai FDA registration is complete**. This makes claim language the highest-risk surface on the site. It also happens to be a brand opportunity: a brand that visibly refuses to overclaim reads as more credible than one that doesn't.

### 6.1 Three tiers of statement

| Tier | Definition | Example | Where allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Compositional fact** | What is verifiably in the product or how it is made | "Each 10 g sachet contains fermented mare's milk powder, vitamin C and omega-3 fatty acids." | Anywhere |
| **B — General mechanism** | Established, non-product-specific physiology, clearly framed as general | "Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation, which supports skin structure." | Anywhere, if attributed to the nutrient and not to Steppe Gut |
| **C — Product efficacy** | A claim that *this product* produces *this outcome* in a person | "Steppe Gut improves skin clarity." | **Nowhere, until registration and substantiation exist.** |

Every sentence on the site must be Tier A or Tier B. If a sentence is Tier C, it does not ship.

### 6.2 Practical rules

- Never place a health outcome in the same sentence as the brand name.
- Attribute mechanism to the *nutrient*, not the *product*: "Vitamin C contributes to…", not "Steppe Gut helps your skin…".
- Never use before/after imagery, testimonials describing health outcomes, or percentage improvements.
- The word "radiance" is brand positioning, not a claim. Keep it in headlines and taglines; never let it appear in a sentence that also describes a physiological outcome.
- The regulatory disclosure block (`01_navigation.md` §7.5) appears in the footer of every page.
- Any statement about research uses hedged, accurate language: "Studies of fermented mare's milk are limited and mostly small. Here is what they have looked at, and what they have not."

### 6.3 The honest-limits pattern

Every science page ends with a short block, styled identically sitewide, headed **What we don't know yet**. This is a required section, not an optional one. It contains 2–4 plain sentences naming genuine open questions.

This is the most valuable trust device on the site. It costs nothing, is legally protective, and no competitor in this category does it.

## 7. CTA language

| Context | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| To a product page | Read the details · See the sachets | Shop now! · Buy today |
| To add to cart | Add to basket | Grab yours · Get it now |
| To an education page | How it works · Read the research · Learn about fermentation | Discover the secret · Find out more (too vague) |
| To the story | Where it comes from · Meet the herders | Our journey |
| Newsletter | Subscribe | Join the tribe · Get exclusive access |
| Contact | Send a message | Get in touch today! |

CTA text is **sentence case**, not Title Case and never ALL CAPS. It is a sentence fragment a person would say.

---

## 8. Tone by page type

| Page type | Register | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Inviting, spare. Almost no persuasion | Short. Every section ≤ 60 words of body copy |
| Product detail | Factual and complete. Answers questions before they're asked | Medium. Density is trust here |
| Science | Explanatory, patient, hedged. Teacher, not salesperson | Long. 600–1,000 words |
| Wellbeing | Warm, practical, useful whether or not you buy | Medium. 400–600 words |
| Our Story | Narrative, concrete, restrained | Long, but broken into chapters |
| FAQ | Direct. Answer in the first sentence, then explain | Short. 40–120 words per answer |
| Legal | Plain-English legal. Correct but readable | As long as required |
| Trade / Press / Careers | Professional and efficient. Still not corporate | Short |

## 9. Bilingual rules (EN / TH)

- Thai is a **first-class language, not a translation layer**. Thai copy is written by a Thai writer against the same brief, not machine-translated from English.
- Thai sets in **Noto Sans Thai**; the display serif (EB Garamond) has no Thai coverage, so Thai headings use Noto Sans Thai 700 at a slightly smaller size (see `03_design_system.md` §9).
- Thai runs **15–25% longer** than English in most cases. Never build a layout that breaks if a heading wraps to two lines.
- Thai needs more line-height. Never share a `line-height` token between scripts.
- Numbers, units, ingredient names and the brand name stay in Latin script in Thai copy.
- The language toggle is in the footer, not the header — it is a preference, not a navigation task, and putting it in the header steals a slot from content.

**Not used as trust signals:** star ratings, review counts, "as seen in" logos, customer photos, follower counts, doctor endorsements. None of these exist honestly pre-launch, and fabricating them would undo everything above.
